Pison is a startup that specializes in neural data collection. Its watch uses electroneurography to detect electrical signals traveling from the brain through the nerves to the muscles. In this project, Pison invited my team to develop new AI features for its existing products.

More about Pison

Through user interviews and market research, we found that Pison was already collecting a wealth of physiological and neural data on an ongoing basis. For users, the real gap was between seeing the data and knowing how to act on it. They could track more and more metrics, yet still struggled to understand what a change meant or what to do about it in the moment.

Existing Solutions

Three major design choices

Action
Over Dashboard

We stopped adding charts and designed the agent to combine multiple metrics into advice users could act on right away. The focus shifted from “What’s my heart rate variability (HRV)?” to “What should I adjust today?”

Direct To User

Health advice is time-sensitive. People are unlikely to open a new app every time their readings change, so we chose to send recommendations by text. A text message has limited space, but it can reach someone at the moment the advice matters.

Context of the
whole day

A poor night’s sleep has different implications on a day off than it does two hours before a job interview. The agent connects to the user’s calendar and considers their schedule alongside their physiological data, profile, and past conversations to decide whether a recommendation makes sense in that moment.
