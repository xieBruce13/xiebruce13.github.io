Context & Problem

In my design research, I use eye tracking to understand which images capture viewers’ attention and whether they engage with the accompanying captions. However, dedicated eye-tracking devices are expensive and time-consuming to set up, making them impractical for quick reviews and limiting my ability to collect data at scale.

Solution

I built a browser-based tool that estimates users’ gaze patterns as they read PDFs on their laptops, generating a heatmap for each page during a reading session. Once sufficient data has been collected across multiple sessions, AI can analyze aggregated attention patterns, identify design issues, and provide actionable feedback.

How the webcam estimates gaze

  1. Find the eyes

    WebGazer crops an image of each eye and records the position of the head.

  2. Learn the screen

    As the reader looks at 17 targets, ridge regression learns to map those features to screen coordinates.

  3. Check the estimate

    Five new targets test accuracy. The dotted line shows how far one prediction falls from its target.
